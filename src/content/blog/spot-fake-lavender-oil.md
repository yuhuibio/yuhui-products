## **Evaluate the Aroma (The Olfactory Test)**
Genuine 100% pure [**lavender oil**](https://www.yuhuibiotec.com/products/lavender-oil/) (*Lavandula angustifolia*) is highly concentrated—containing over 70 times the aromatic compounds of the raw plant.

**The Real Deal**: It features a fresh, natural, and multi‑layered scent with subtle herbaceous and earthy undertones. When you open the bottle, it should never have a sharp, chemical‑like piercing odor.

**The Fake**: Adulterated oils often smell overwhelmingly sweet, flat, or artificial because they rely on synthetic linalyl acetate or cheap fragrance additives.

## **The Balloon Test (Testing Penetration Power)**
High‑concentration essential oils possess powerful natural solvency and rapid penetration capabilities.

**How to test**: Blow up a regular balloon and apply a tiny drop of the essential oil onto its surface.

**The Result**: Pure, high‑potency [**lavender oil**](https://www.yuhuibiotec.com/products/lavender-oil/) will typically cause the balloon to pop within seconds due to its strong interaction with the latex material.

## **Check Volatility and Residue (The Paper Test)**
Most pure essential oils have a high evaporation rate.

**How to test**: Place a single drop of essential oil on a clean sheet of white paper and allow it to dry completely.

**The Result**: Pure [**lavender oil**](https://www.yuhuibiotec.com/products/lavender-oil/) will evaporate completely without leaving any greasy oil rings or residue, while leaving a pleasant, lingering botanical fragrance behind. If the paper stays oily or greasy hours later, it has likely been cut with carrier oils (like vegetable or mineral oil).

## **Observe Skin Absorption and Dispersion**
Because of their micro‑molecular structure, high‑grade essential oils absorb rapidly into the skin.

**Skin Test**: Rub a drop onto the back of your hand. A pure extract absorbs quickly without a heavy, greasy feeling.

**Water Dispersion**: When dropped into water, pure essential oil should disperse cleanly and rapidly. Large, heavy oil droplets forming on the water surface usually indicate carrier oil dilution (such as rapeseed or peanut oil).
